My Patterns and Observations

My Phenomenon Brainstorm

The phenomenon I decided to explore is whether genetic counselors are objective enough when helping families make decisions about genetic conditions. I became interested in this after looking through my observations and noticing how often difficult decisions involving genetic disabilities came up. Genetic counselors are supposed to give families information and help them understand their options, but the decisions they are involved in can be extremely personal and emotional. This made me wonder how possible it actually is for a genetic counselor to remain completely objective. Out of the different patterns I noticed, this stood out to me because the way information is presented could potentially affect how a family views a genetic condition and what they decide to do.

One of the strongest patterns I noticed was the possibility that genetic counselors could have their own biases when discussing genetic conditions. My current thinking is that even if a counselor is trying to be completely neutral, their personal experiences or views of certain disabilities could affect the way they explain something. At the same time, I realize that I am making an assumption by thinking that bias is a major problem without knowing how genetic counselors are actually trained to avoid it. This leaves me with questions like: How are genetic counselors taught to stay objective? How much can their wording influence a family's decision? Is being completely objective even possible when discussing something as serious as a child's future?
